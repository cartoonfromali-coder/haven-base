Mattress Care Guide

How Often Should You Rotate a Mattress?

Regular mattress care can help you use your sleeping surface more evenly. Before rotating yours, however, check what the manufacturer recommends.

Quick answer Many mattresses can benefit from being rotated periodically, but the correct schedule depends on the mattress design. Always follow the manufacturer's care instructions and check whether the mattress is designed to be rotated.

Why Rotate a Mattress?

The same areas of a mattress can receive pressure night after night. Rotating the mattress can change where those pressure points occur and may help distribute normal use more evenly.

However, not every mattress should be rotated in the same way. Some designs have specific orientations or comfort layers that mean the manufacturer's instructions should take priority.

There Is No Universal Schedule

Check first

Read the care instructions supplied with the mattress before changing its position.

Rotate as advised

Follow the manufacturer's recommended frequency rather than relying on a generic timetable.

Stay consistent

If rotation is recommended, keeping track of when you last did it makes the routine easier.

Rotation Is Different From Flipping

Rotating usually means turning the mattress around so the head end becomes the foot end. Flipping means turning the mattress over.

These are not interchangeable. A mattress may be designed to be rotated but not flipped, particularly when it has a dedicated sleeping surface or layered construction.

Important: Never assume that a mattress can be flipped simply because it can be rotated. Check the manufacturer's instructions for the specific mattress.

When Should You Check the Mattress?

Mattress care is also a good opportunity to inspect the sleeping surface. Look for visible changes, unusual wear or anything that suggests the mattress needs attention.

Also check that the mattress remains correctly positioned on its bed base after moving it.

Consider the Mattress Construction

Different mattress types can have different care requirements. Spring systems, memory foam layers and pillow-top constructions may respond differently to movement and pressure.

This is why general advice should never replace the instructions supplied with the mattress you actually own.

Make Rotation Easier

Mattresses can be heavy, especially in larger sizes. If you need to rotate one, clear the surrounding area first and make sure you have enough room to move safely.

For larger or heavier mattresses, having another person help can make the task easier and safer.

Mattress Care Checklist

  • Read the manufacturer's care instructions.
  • Confirm that your mattress is designed to be rotated.
  • Do not confuse rotation with flipping.
  • Keep track of when you last rotated the mattress.
  • Inspect the mattress when moving it.
  • Make sure the mattress sits correctly on its base afterwards.
  • Use help when moving a large or heavy mattress.
