How to Measure Your Bedroom for a Padded Bed

Accurate measurements can help you choose a padded bed that fits comfortably without making the room difficult to move around.

Quick answer Measure the available floor area first, then account for the padded bed's external dimensions, headboard, bedside furniture, doors and walking space. Do not rely only on the mattress size because an upholstered bed can extend beyond it.

Start With the Room, Not the Bed

It is tempting to begin by choosing a bed size and then work out whether it fits. A better approach is to measure the bedroom first and understand what space is genuinely available.

Measure the length and width of the room, but also note doors, windows, wardrobes, radiators and other fixed features. These details can affect where the bed can realistically sit.

Four Measurements Worth Taking

1 Room dimensions

Measure the usable length and width of the bedroom rather than estimating from the room plan.

2 Available bed area

Identify the section of floor where the bed can actually be positioned.

3 Furniture clearance

Allow room for bedside tables, wardrobes, drawers and other furniture to remain usable.

4 Access route

Check doorways, stairs and turns if the finished bed will need to be carried into the room.

Remember the External Bed Dimensions

A mattress size does not always tell you the full footprint of an upholstered bed. The base and headboard can add width or length beyond the mattress itself.

When comparing padded beds, look for the stated external measurements where available. These are more useful for bedroom planning than relying on a nominal mattress size alone.

Planning tip: If you have a tight space, mark the proposed bed footprint on the floor using removable tape. This gives you a quick visual check of how much room will remain around the bed.

Measure Around Existing Furniture

Think about how you use the rest of the bedroom. A bed may physically fit while still making wardrobes difficult to open or leaving very little space beside the mattress.

Check the position of bedside tables, wardrobes, chest drawers and bedroom doors. If a wardrobe door opens towards the bed, include that movement area in your planning.

Do Not Forget the Headboard

The headboard can change the visual and physical footprint of a padded bed. A taller or more substantial design may need additional wall space and can affect the position of bedside furniture.

Measure the wall where the headboard will sit and check for sockets, switches, windows or other features that could interfere with the final position.

Check the Route Into the Bedroom

Room measurements are only part of the process. The bed components also need to reach the bedroom.

Measure narrow doorways, stair turns and awkward corners where necessary. This is particularly useful in older properties where access can be less straightforward than the bedroom itself.

Final Measurement Checklist

  • Measure the bedroom's usable floor area.
  • Record the position of doors, windows and fixed features.
  • Check the padded bed's external dimensions.
  • Allow space for bedside tables and other furniture.
  • Consider wardrobe and door opening space.
  • Measure the wall available for the headboard.
  • Check the delivery route into the bedroom.
  • Leave comfortable space for movement around the bed.
