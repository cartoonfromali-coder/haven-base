Kids Bedroom Planning

How to Fit a Kids Bed Around Existing Furniture

A new bed does not have to mean replacing the whole bedroom. With careful measurements and planning, you can work a kids bed around furniture you already own.

Quick answer Start by measuring the complete bed footprint and then plan around wardrobes, drawers, desks and walking routes. Always allow enough space for doors and drawers to open properly.

Measure Before Moving Anything

The easiest way to create problems is to buy the bed first and try to make the existing furniture fit afterwards.

Measure the bedroom, the proposed bed and all major furniture pieces before deciding where everything should go. Include doors, windows, radiators and other fixed features.

Identify the Three Main Zones

Sleeping zone

Allow enough space around the bed for getting in, getting out and changing bedding.

Storage zone

Make sure wardrobes and drawers remain accessible once the bed is positioned.

Movement zone

Keep a practical route through the bedroom rather than filling every available area with furniture.

Check the Bed's External Dimensions

A mattress measurement is not necessarily the same as the finished bed measurement. Headboards, side rails and other frame elements can increase the overall footprint.

Use the manufacturer's external dimensions when planning the room.

Planning tip: Mark the bed footprint on the floor using removable tape. This gives you a simple way to test whether existing furniture still works around it.

Keep Wardrobes and Drawers Usable

A wardrobe may physically fit beside the bed but still be difficult to use if the doors cannot open fully.

The same applies to drawers. Measure their open position and make sure the bed does not create a collision point.

Think About the Desk

Many children's bedrooms also need space for homework, drawing or other activities. A desk should remain accessible without creating a narrow passage between it and the bed.

If the bedroom is compact, consider which furniture needs the most frequent access and give that area priority.

Do Not Block the Bedroom Door

The bed should not interfere with the bedroom entrance. Check the door swing and the route from the doorway to the main areas of the room.

This is particularly important in smaller bedrooms where moving the bed by only a small amount can significantly change the usable layout.

Use the Walls Carefully

Where possible, placing larger furniture along suitable walls can help keep the centre of the room clearer. But do not assume every wall is interchangeable.

Windows, radiators, sockets and doors can all affect where the bed and furniture should go.

Try the Layout Before Buying

If you are unsure, create a simple floor plan. You do not need specialist software; a sheet of paper with approximate measurements can be enough to compare different arrangements.

You can also mark furniture footprints on the floor temporarily to test walking routes and access.

Kids Bedroom Layout Checklist

  • Measure the complete kids bed footprint.
  • Measure existing wardrobes and drawers.
  • Check doors and drawers can open fully.
  • Leave a practical walking route.
  • Consider desks and study areas.
  • Check the bedroom door swing.
  • Consider windows, radiators and sockets.
  • Test the proposed layout before buying.
